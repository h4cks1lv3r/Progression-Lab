# Progression Lab Product Voice

## Purpose

Progression Lab should sound like a serious training culture built around evidence, ownership, and steady improvement. The voice is mature, direct, intelligent, and encouraging, with a restrained amount of current energy.

The target reaction is:

> This feels current, capable, and like a culture I would actually train with.

A younger adult should not find the app stiff. A mature adult should not find it embarrassing.

## Core principles

### Competent

- Use precise training language.
- Explain what changed and why it matters.
- Separate measured facts, associations, and hypotheses.
- Never make medical, diagnostic, or unsupported causal claims.

### Direct

- Use short sentences and strong verbs.
- Put the result or next action first.
- Remove filler, corporate phrasing, and unnecessary apology language.

### Grounded confidence

Celebrate earned progress without exaggeration.

Good:

> New bench press best. You added 5 lb at the same rep count.

Avoid:

> You are unstoppable! Nothing can hold you back!

### Mature encouragement

- Reward consistency without guilt.
- Never shame missed sessions, food, supplements, bodyweight, or incomplete logs.
- Preserve user agency.

Good:

> Session missed. Your program position is unchanged.

Avoid:

> You broke your streak. Get back on track now.

### Restrained cultural energy

Use a light modern phrase only when it adds energy and remains clear to a broad adult audience.

Approved examples:

- Locked in
- Strong work
- Clean session
- That moved
- Built, not guessed
- Run the next test
- Signal, not noise
- The trend is worth watching

Avoid:

- forced meme language
- excessive slang
- `bro`, `fam`, `rizz`, `slay`, `goated`, `no cap`, and similar default copy
- repeated exclamation marks
- profanity in default app text
- language likely to feel dated quickly

Essential instructions must never depend on slang.

## Cultural foundation

Progression Lab is built around:

- training as an experiment
- evidence over guessing
- consistency over guilt
- progress that can be inspected
- strength and athleticism together
- ownership of personal data
- curiosity about what moves the needle

Recurring brand language may include:

```text
Test. Train. Transform.
Run the next test.
Built, not guessed.
What moved the needle?
Signal, not noise.
Your data. Your call.
Progress under review.
```

Do not force the laboratory metaphor into every sentence.

## Terminology

Use these terms consistently:

| Concept | Preferred term |
|---|---|
| Strength training event | Workout |
| Athletic training event | Session |
| Prescribed loaded set | Working set |
| Preparation set | Warm-up set |
| Load added to bodyweight | Added weight |
| Load removed by a machine or band | Assistance |
| Major Strength section | Phase |
| Repeating training segment | Cycle |
| A complete pass through a program | Program run |
| Supplement, meal, hydration, and recovery logging | Daily Inputs |
| Deterministic analysis | Lab Core |
| Optional Gemini explanation | AI Analysis |

Do not alternate randomly between plan, program, routine, block, and cycle when they refer to the same object.

## Tone by context

### Onboarding

Calm, capable, and welcoming.

> Start here. Home keeps your next workout, current program, and Daily Inputs in one place.

> Two programs. Separate progress. One training system.

### Before training

Focused and low-noise.

> Upper Body A is ready.

> Your warm-up is calculated. Working sets begin at 185 lb.

### During training

Operational and concise.

> Set logged.

> Rest timer started.

> One working set remains.

Use a higher-energy line only after a real achievement:

> Locked in. New best at this rep range.

### After training

Specific and earned.

> Workout complete. 18 working sets logged in 47 minutes.

> Clean work. Lower Body A is next.

### Progress and records

Evidence-led.

> New estimated strength best: 2.4% above your previous top set.

> Volume is higher across four matched workouts. Keep watching the trend.

### Daily Inputs

Neutral and nonjudgmental.

> 280 mg caffeine logged today.

> Creatine: 5 of the last 7 days.

### The Lab

Analytical and honest.

> Caffeine timing is associated with stronger matched workouts in this sample. Sleep and program phase may explain part of the difference.

> Not enough matched workouts yet. Keep logging before changing your routine.

### Empty states

Useful, never scolding.

> No matched workouts yet. Complete a few comparable sessions to start seeing patterns.

> No supplements logged today. Add one when there is something worth tracking.

### Errors

Calm, specific, and actionable.

> That set was not saved. Your previous data is unchanged. Try again.

> Gemini is not available on this device. Lab Core is still ready.

Avoid `Something went wrong` when a more useful explanation is available.

### Destructive actions

Explicit and sober.

> This removes the unfinished draft. Completed workouts and records stay intact.

> Archive this exercise? It will leave normal pickers, but its history will remain.

## AI Analysis rules

Gemini output must follow the same voice.

The system prompt and output validation must require:

- mature, direct language
- no forced slang
- no diagnosis or medical prescription
- no causal claim from observational data
- no body shaming or guilt
- no supplement hype or unsafe dose advice
- visible sample size, date range, confidence, and confounders
- no invented numbers
- no claims that are not traceable to deterministic Lab evidence
- at most one light cultural phrase when the context earns it

Reject or replace output that violates critical safety or evidence rules.

## Accessibility and localization

- Keep button labels literal and short.
- Do not use all caps for long sentences.
- Do not encode a critical instruction in humor or slang.
- Avoid idioms that translate poorly.
- Give icons descriptive screen-reader labels.
- Keep privacy, safety, error, and destructive-action copy serious.

## Review checklist

For every feature or release:

1. Inventory new and changed user-facing strings.
2. Check terminology against this guide.
3. Remove guilt, body shame, forced hype, and vague errors.
4. Verify claims and uncertainty language.
5. Review Gemini prompt changes.
6. Check accessibility and localization readiness.
7. Review the copy with both younger and mature adult testers.

## Quick transformations

Too sterile:

> Workout completion successful.

Use:

> Workout complete. Your progress is saved.

Too forced:

> Bro, you absolutely cooked that workout. No cap.

Use:

> Strong work. You beat your previous top set.

Too vague:

> Great job!

Use:

> All prescribed working sets are complete.

Too harsh:

> You failed to complete your workout.

Use:

> Workout ended early. Logged sets are saved.

Too promotional:

> Creatine is supercharging your gains.

Use:

> Strength was higher during periods with consistent creatine logging. Other factors may have contributed.
